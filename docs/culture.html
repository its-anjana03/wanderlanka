Come hungry

Six things to eat and drink before you leave. Say yes when someone offers seconds; they will offer anyway.

Rice and curry

Not one dish but a table: rice with four to eight small curries, a sambol and a crisp. Lunch is the main serving. Eat with your right hand if you like.

Hoppers (appa)

Bowl-shaped pancakes of fermented rice batter, crisp at the edge, soft in the middle. An egg hopper has an egg cooked into it.

Kottu

Chopped flatbread fried on a hot plate with vegetables, egg or meat. You hear the blades before you see the stall.

Pol sambol

Fresh coconut ground with chilli, onion and lime. It goes with almost everything.

Kiribath

Rice cooked in coconut milk and cut into diamonds. The first food of the New Year and of any new beginning.

Ceylon tea

Grown from sea level to 2,000 metres. High-grown tea from Nuwara Eliya and Uva is lighter. Low-grown tea is dark and strong.

Festivals by the moon

Fire, elephants and a million paper lanterns. Dates follow the moon and shift each year. Check the year's calendar before you plan around one.

  1. January

    Thai Pongal

    Tamil harvest thanksgiving. Milk rice is boiled until it overflows.

  2. April

    Sinhala and Tamil New Year

    On 13 and 14 April the whole country goes home. Rituals follow set auspicious times.

  3. May

    Vesak

    The full moon marking the Buddha's birth, enlightenment and passing. Lanterns and free food stalls fill the streets.

  4. June

    Poson

    The full moon that remembers the arrival of Buddhism at Mihintale.

  5. July or August

    Kandy Esala Perahera

    Ten nights of processions carrying the casket of the Tooth Relic.

  6. August

    Nallur festival

    Twenty-five days at the great Murugan temple in Jaffna.

  7. October or November

    Deepavali

    The Hindu festival of lights.

  8. Every full moon

    Poya

    A public holiday each month. Temples are full and alcohol is not sold.

Three ways to drum

You will hear the drums before you see the dancers. Sri Lanka has three classical traditions, each with its own drum.

Kandyan

උඩරට

The hill-country style seen in the Kandy Perahera. Ves dancers wear a silver headdress and beaded harness and move to the geta beraya, a drum with two different skins.

Low country

පහතරට

The southern coastal style of masks and healing rituals, driven by the long yak beraya. This is where the Sanni and Raksha masks belong.

Sabaragamuwa

සබරගමුව

The style of the country around Ratnapura, tied to the worship of the god Saman, guardian of Sri Pada. Its drum is the davula.

Eighteen faces of illness

In the Daha Ata Sanniya, a healing ritual of the southern low country, each illness has a face. A dancer wears the mask of the demon behind the sickness. The demon is called in, fed, teased by the drummer and sent away.

  • Amukkuඅමුක්කු

    Vomiting and stomach sickness

    Green-faced, tongue hanging out.
  • Abuthaඅභූත

    Madness with no spirit behind it

    Wide eyes over a clamped mouth.
  • Buthaභූත

    Madness caused by spirits

    Dark face ringed with flames.
  • Bihiriබිහිරි

    Deafness

    A cobra hood covers one ear.
  • Devaදේව

    Epidemic diseases

    The one calm, handsome face on the wall.
  • Gediගෙඩි

    Boils and skin diseases

    Cheeks and brow covered in boils.
  • Gini Jalaගිනි ජල

    Malaria and high fevers

    Fever red, crowned with fire.
  • Goluගොළු

    Dumbness

    Mouth open, no sound, no teeth.
  • Gulmaගුල්ම

    Parasitic worms and stomach diseases

    Pale and tight-lipped.
  • Jalaජල

    Cholera and chills

    Cold blue with chattering teeth.
  • Kanaකණ

    Blindness

    One eye shut, the other empty.
  • Koraකෝර

    Lameness and paralysis

    The whole face pulled to one side.
  • Maruමරු

    Delirium and death

    Bone white under red flames.
  • Nagaනාග

    Bad dreams about snakes

    Three cobras rise from the forehead.
  • Pissuපිස්සු

    Temporary insanity

    Eyes that do not match, tongue out.
  • Pithපිත්

    Bilious diseases

    Painted the yellow of bile.
  • Slesmaස්ලේෂ්ම

    Phlegm and epilepsy

    Slack mouth, one eye rolled back.
  • Vathaවාත

    Flatulence and rheumatism

    Stiff, with a crooked jaw.

The list of eighteen differs between villages and books. To see real masks, visit the mask museums and workshops in Ambalangoda on the south-west coast.

The moonstone

At the foot of temple stairs in Anuradhapura and Polonnaruwa lies a carved half circle of stone called a sandakada pahana (සඳකඩ පහණ). In the most common reading, stepping over it means crossing the whole cycle of life.

  1. Flames

    The outer band of fire is read as the endless burning of desire that keeps the world turning.

  2. Four animals

    Elephant, horse, lion and bull walk in a line. They are read as birth, old age, sickness and death, the four things no life avoids.

  3. The creeper

    A twisting vine called liyawel stands for craving, which winds around everything.

  4. Geese

    The hansa, a goose said to be able to separate milk from water, stands for telling what matters from what does not.

  5. The lotus

    At the centre a half lotus: the stillness reached when the outer bands are left behind. You step over all of it to enter the shrine.

This is the reading proposed by the archaeologist Senarath Paranavitana, and the one most often taught. Other scholars read the bands differently. In Polonnaruwa the bull was removed from the design.
