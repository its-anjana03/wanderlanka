Horton Plains

A cold, windy plateau above 2,000 metres that ends in a sheer drop called World's End.

Region
Hill Country
Time needed

Walk to the edge of the world

Three reasons it belongs on your route.

  1. 01

    At World's End the ground falls away for about 870 metres. On a clear morning you can see to the south coast.

  2. 02

    The cloud forest and grassland are unlike anything else on the island, and sambar deer graze beside the road.

  3. 03

    The 9-kilometre loop also passes Baker's Falls.

How to see it

Go

Be at the gate by 6. The view at World's End is usually lost in cloud by about 9.30.

Know

It is cold at dawn, often under 10°C. Bring a warm layer and a rain jacket.

Skip

Arriving after 9. You will pay the same fee to look at a white wall of mist.

The story so far

The plateau is the source of three of the island's major rivers: the Mahaweli, the Kelani and the Walawe. The British named it in 1834 after a governor, Sir Robert Wilmot-Horton. Its older Sinhala name is Maha Eliya, the great open plain.

It became a national park in 1988. In 2010 UNESCO listed it with the Peak Wilderness and the Knuckles range as the Central Highlands, for the number of plants and animals found nowhere else.

Getting there

About 1 hour by road from Nuwara Eliya or 1.5 hours from Haputale. There is no public transport to the gate, so hire a van or tuk-tuk.

Good manners here

  • Plastic bags and bottle labels are removed at the entrance check.
  • Stay on the trail. The grassland is fragile.
