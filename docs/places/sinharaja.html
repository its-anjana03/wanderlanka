The last great rainforest

Three reasons it belongs on your route.

  1. 01

    More than 60 percent of its trees grow nowhere else on Earth.

  2. 02

    Birds here feed in mixed flocks of a dozen species moving together through the canopy.

  3. 03

    It is quiet. You walk, and the forest comes to you slowly.

How to see it

Go

Start at opening time with your guide. Bird activity drops by mid-morning.

Know

There are leeches, more after rain. Wear leech socks and it is a non-issue.

Skip

Expecting big animals. This forest is about trees, birds, lizards and frogs.

The story so far

Sinharaja means lion king. It survived because its steep ridges were hard to reach. In the 1970s the state began mechanised logging here for plywood. Public protest and scientific study stopped it in 1977.

It was declared a biosphere reserve in 1978 and a World Heritage site in 1988. The villages on its edge still tap kitul palms for treacle inside the buffer zone.

Getting there

Main entrances are at Kudawa (north-west, from Ratnapura or Colombo) and Pitadeniya near Deniyaya (south, from Galle or Mirissa). Roads are slow. Allow 3 hours from the coast.

Good manners here

  • A Forest Department guide goes with every group.
  • Take every wrapper back out with you.
