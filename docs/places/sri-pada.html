One mountain, one footprint, and four religions that climb it together at night.

Climb all night for one sunrise

Three reasons it belongs on your route.

  1. 01

    You climb by lamplight among grandparents, monks and children, and reach the top for sunrise.

  2. 02

    At dawn the mountain throws a perfect triangular shadow across the clouds.

  3. 03

    It is the rare great sight that is still a pilgrimage first.

How to see it

Go

In season, start around 2 in the morning. The path is lit and tea stalls are open all the way.

Know

About 5,500 steps. Your knees will feel the way down more than the way up. It is cold and windy on top.

Skip

Poya days and weekends, when the last stretch can queue for hours. Out of season the lights are off, so do not go at night without a guide.

The story so far

Near the 2,243-metre summit is a hollow in the rock shaped like a footprint. Buddhists hold it to be the Buddha's. Hindus call the mountain Sivanolipatha Malai, after Shiva. Many Muslims and Christians link the mark to Adam, which gave it the English name Adam's Peak.

People have climbed it for well over a thousand years. Marco Polo and Ibn Battuta both wrote about it. Its other old name, Samanala Kanda, means butterfly mountain.

The surrounding Peak Wilderness is part of the Central Highlands World Heritage listing.

Getting there

The main route starts at Nallathanniya (Dalhousie), about 1.5 hours from Hatton station on the hill line.

Good manners here

  • This is a holy place. Dress modestly and keep your voice down at the summit.
  • First-time pilgrims are greeted on the way. A smile and "Karunawai" is the reply.
