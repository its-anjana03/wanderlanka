03

Money

Rupees in your pocket, cards for the big sites.

The currency is the Sri Lankan rupee (LKR). ATMs are common in towns and accept foreign cards. Carry cash for tuk-tuks, small restaurants, temples and markets. Major sites price foreign tickets in US dollars but usually accept rupees or cards.

A 10 percent service charge is normally added in restaurants and hotels. Beyond that, small tips for drivers, guides and hotel staff are welcome.

04

Getting around

The slow train is the best seat on the island.

Train. Slow, cheap and beautiful, above all between Kandy and Ella. Reserved seats sell out, so book on the official reservation site as soon as sales open. Unreserved second and third class tickets are sold at the station on the day.

Tuk-tuk. In cities use a ride app such as PickMe or Uber, which fixes the price. Elsewhere agree the fare before you get in.

Car with driver. The common way to cover the Cultural Triangle. Distances look short but roads are slow. Plan on about 40 km an hour away from the expressways.

Bus. Goes everywhere and costs very little. Fast, loud and crowded.

05

Temple manners

A few simple rules, and every temple door is open to you.

  • Cover shoulders and knees. Take off shoes and hats before you enter.
  • Never pose with your back to a Buddha statue. Selfies with statues have led to arrests.
  • A visible tattoo of the Buddha can cause serious trouble, including being refused entry to the country. Keep it covered.
  • Women should not touch monks or hand things to them directly.
  • Walk around stupas clockwise.
  • On Poya (full moon) days alcohol is not sold anywhere.
06

Safety and common scams

Sri Lanka is famously kind to visitors. Here are the rare exceptions.

  • "It is closed today." A friendly stranger says your destination is shut and offers another. It is almost never shut.
  • Gem deals. No stranger has a special price on sapphires for you to resell at home.
  • Spice gardens. Free tours end in a shop with very high prices. You are free to leave.
  • The sea. Rip currents are strong on many beaches, and few have lifeguards. Ask locally before you swim.
  • Roads. Buses overtake hard. Cross with care and avoid driving after dark.

Sri Lankans are widely and rightly known for kindness to visitors. These are the exceptions.

07

Wildlife, done well

See them wild, and leave them that way.

  • Do not ride elephants or visit places that chain them for photographs. See them wild at Udawalawe, Minneriya or Yala.
  • On safari, ask for a driver who keeps distance and does not race to sightings.
  • For whale watching, choose an operator that keeps away from the animals and does not chase.
  • Do not feed monkeys or wild elephants on the roadside. Fed animals become dangerous and are often killed.
  • Turtle hatcheries vary widely. Avoid any that keep turtles in tanks for handling.
